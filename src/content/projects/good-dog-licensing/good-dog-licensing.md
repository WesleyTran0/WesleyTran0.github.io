Good Dog Licensing is a free, student-run music sync service at Northeastern University, connecting
independent musicians with media makers seeking music.

As part of [Sandbox](https://www.sandboxnu.com/), Northeastern's student-led software consultancy, I
contributed to [Good Dog's website redesign](https://good-dog-licensing.vercel.app/), working across
the site to build a visually polished and performant user experience.

On the frontend, I worked with our designers to translate their wireframes into clean, polished
interfaces that matched the platform's new visual direction. On the backend, I built tRPC endpoints
to fetch pre-fill values from users' previous submissions, streamlining the music submission process
for returning artists and media makers.

This project reinforced my ability to work on full-stack applications in a team
environment—coordinating with designers on implementation details, writing backend logic that other
developers could easily extend, and delivering features that tied both halves together into a
cohesive user experience.

Beyond the technical work, contributing to Good Dog gave me insight into the music licensing
industry and the legal frameworks that make synchronization possible. Working alongside
[Professor Herlihy](https://camd.northeastern.edu/people/david-herlihy/)'s Music Licensing for Media
course and Green Line Records meant understanding not just how to build features, but why they
mattered to real users navigating copyright, attribution, and creative collaboration.
